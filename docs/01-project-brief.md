# 01 · Project brief

**Client:** TripView
**Commission:** UX research, interaction design and prototype evaluation for a transfer assistance feature on Sydney public transport
**Scope of delivery:** clickable web demo, final high-fidelity screens, research and evaluation materials

## Background and goal

TripView wanted to explore a transfer assistance feature called **Transfer Coach**. Before departure, riders can already see routes and timetables. During the few minutes of a transfer they are asking a different set of questions: Can I still make it? Which way do I go? If I cannot, what do I do now?

The commission was to design a mobile experience that can be read quickly on the move. Riders should be able to judge the time left in a transfer, find the right exit and stop sign, and continue the journey after a delay, a missed service or a missed stop. The design also had to separate two questions that are easy to confuse: can I catch the next service, and will I arrive at my destination on time.

## Target users and demo scenario

The primary user is a commuter who makes at least one transfer in Sydney and checks a phone for timetables. They may know the daily route, but a sudden delay, a tight connection or an unfamiliar station path still leaves them needing a clear next step.

**Demo trip:** Green Square, Train T2, Central Station, Stand C for Bus 343, Main Hall at Lakeside Hall, arriving for an event that starts at 10:30.

The normal flow runs from planning to arrival. Exception flows cover a delayed train, a missed train, a delayed bus, a missed bus and recovery after riding past the right stop.

## Requirements and acceptance

| Capability | Requirement | What a reviewer should be able to see |
|---|---|---|
| Planning and route choice | Show origin, destination, latest arrival time, modes and route preferences (balanced, fastest, step-free) | The rider can compare candidate routes by estimated arrival and confirm one before starting |
| Transfer judgement | Put next-service countdown, walking time inside the station and spare time in one view. Show arrival at the destination and minutes before the event separately | The rider can tell "bus departs in 6 min, 3 min walk, 3 min to spare" apart from "arrive 10:20, 10 min before the event" |
| Station guidance | Landmarks, direction graphics and short sentences from platform to stand. Show the faster exit and the lift route. Offer a way to re-confirm position from a landmark | The rider knows which exit to take, when to go up or use the lift, where to turn and how to confirm arrival at Stand C |
| Delays and rerouting | Show the size of the delay, the updated departure and the effect on arrival. Offer waiting or switching | When Bus 343 is late, arrival already reflects the delay, and the rider can compare waiting with switching to Bus 410 from Stand D |
| Missed services and wrong stop | A recovery flow for each: missed train, missed bus, wrong stop | After riding past Central, the rider can see a way to return on T5 and continue the transfer |
| Progress and arrival | Trip overview, vehicle status and stop list distinguishing walking, waiting, riding, transferring and arrival | On the bus the rider can see the boarding stop, next stop and get-off stop; after getting off, an arrival confirmation appears |

The interface is in English. Because riders scan while moving, current status, key times and the next action take priority. Delays and missed services use different states and wording. When a delay affects the whole trip, the impact is explained on the current trip screen so riders do not need to return to an alerts page.

## Deliverables

1. **Research and design rationale.** Desk research, interviews and affinity map, persona, journey map and concept exploration, explaining why the design focuses on transfer judgement, station guidance and recovery.
2. **Prototypes and demo.** Low-fidelity sketches, mid-fidelity flow, a clickable web demo and final high-fidelity screens. All services, routes and delays use demo data.
3. **Evaluation and iteration record.** Heuristic evaluation, scenario-based usability testing and experience evaluation, recording where riders hesitated, what they misread and the matching design changes. Three checks were prioritised: can the rider say what time they will arrive, how many minutes before the event, and which stage of the trip they are in.
